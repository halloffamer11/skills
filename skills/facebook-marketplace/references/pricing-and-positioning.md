# Pricing and positioning

Turn seller facts, media, product evidence, competitor observations, and seller
priorities into a defensible price proposal and a way to present the item.
Every output here is a proposal until the seller approves it; only the seller
sets `pricing.approved`.

## Price

Produce, at the precision the evidence supports:

- **Observed range**: comparable asking prices, the relevant differences, and
  whether any completed-sale evidence exists. Never present an asking price as
  proof of demand, value, or a sale.
- **Recommended asking price or range**, adjusted for confirmed condition,
  included items, proof, warranty, availability, and replacement price.
- **Rationale and confidence**: the few facts that move the number and the
  limits on it (sparse results, uncertain variant, wide area). A thin sample
  supports a broad, qualified range.
- **Alternatives** when seller priorities differ, such as a faster-sale and a
  wait-for-the-right-buyer price, only where evidence supports a difference.
- **Negotiation** (an asking position, an adjustment path, a floor) only when
  the seller asks. Private floors stay in the record.

## Positioning

Lead with what a buyer can verify, in this order of preference: a product
strength that applies to the confirmed item; evidence of this unit's condition
or a stated test result; a precise included-items list with material
omissions; a real convenience the seller offers (set through the form's
delivery settings, not the copy); a competitive gap the seller can close with
better identification, photos, condition proof, testing, or accessory clarity.

Never turn a model capability into proof this unit works, or a competitor's
vague listing into a reason to overstate this one.

## Proposal to the seller

Default to a compact conversational proposal, not a report:

1. Recommended price.
2. Proposed title.
3. Proposed description.
4. Photo guidance for the most material remaining uncertainty.
5. The one next decision or question.

Add rationale only when it explains the recommendation or the seller asks.
Observations, limits, alternatives, sources, and provenance stay in the record.

## Effort calibration

If project configuration sets an improvement threshold, recommend extra seller
work only when market evidence suggests the gain exceeds it, for example when
nearby comparable listings that document an accessory, test, or condition detail
the seller can also verify ask meaningfully more. Do not estimate seller time or
compute per-task ROI. Below the threshold, proceed with the evidence on hand, or
mention the improvement without recommending it.
