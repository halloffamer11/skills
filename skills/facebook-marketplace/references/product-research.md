# Product research

Turn the item's identity, the seller's evidence, and research access into
traceable product evidence and a buyer decision model. Research never upgrades
an unobserved, untested, or unconfirmed fact into a listing claim.

## Identify first

Start from the strongest identity evidence: a readable label, model number,
SKU, manual, packaging, or seller statement, then an OEM manual or support page
tied to it. Match brand, model, generation, and variant only as precisely as
the evidence allows. Visual resemblance is an inference; keep competing
variants open when they would change compatibility, value, or expectations, and
research them only far enough to find the material difference. Fit the
questions to the item; there is no product-specific checklist.

## Use each source for what it can support

- **OEM specifications and support** lead on model identification, specs,
  compatibility, features, and warranty terms, scoped to the exact model and
  region. They do not show real-world reliability or every historical bundle.
- **Independent reviews** show measured performance and trade-offs. Prefer
  transparent methods and a matching model; note firmware or version limits.
- **Owner forums** show recurring concerns and buyer vocabulary. A post is an
  attributed report, not a fact; look for independent corroboration.
- **Retailers** give availability, configurations, and replacement-price
  context. They are offers, not completed sales or proof of what this seller
  includes.
- **General web** is for discovery and archival or regional naming; verify
  anything consequential with a stronger source.

Cite the exact page consulted, never a search-results page.

## What to establish

- **Features**: the OEM's stated capability, prerequisites, and limits;
  independent sources when the question is how it performs. Never infer that
  this unit has the feature enabled, working, or with its accessory.
- **Compatibility**: the counterpart, standard, region, firmware, or adapter
  that matters, and whether the source says compatible, conditionally
  compatible, or unsupported. No blanket promises.
- **Recurring owner concerns**: the pattern, affected versions, how many
  independent reports, and whether it can be checked on this unit. An
  unresolved report stays an attributed concern, not a defect.
- **Replacement-price context**: several current offers with currency, region,
  condition (new, refurbished, open-box), bundle, and date. This is valuation
  context, not what local used units sell for.

## Claim record

Keep one row per material claim in the listing record's Research section:

| Claim ID | URL | Source type | Access date (YYYY-MM-DD) | Supported claim and scope | Confidence and reason | Conflicts or limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

When sources answer different questions, keep both: an OEM water-resistance
rating and owner reports of failures after a use pattern are a spec and an
attributed experience, and neither proves this unit's condition. When sources
conflict on the same claim, prefer the one most authoritative for it, record
the disagreement, and keep buyer copy uncertain until it is resolved or
immaterial.

## Buyer decision model

For this item, identify what buyers compare, the few attributes that change
usefulness, risk, or replacement cost, the trust signals the seller can
truthfully show, recurring objections and whether they apply to this unit, the
technical facts that need a plain-language consequence, and the photos or tests
that would change identification, valuation, safe use, or comparison. Use the
model to steer research, media selection, pricing, and copy; it is not a
feature inventory.

When translating a spec, keep the exact fact, its scope, and its source, and
add a consequence only when evidence supports it. A rated maximum is not
guaranteed performance, and a model capability is not a claim about this unit.

## When to stop

Add sources only while they could change identification, valuation,
positioning, safety or compatibility risk, or the next evidence request. Then
stop and record the remaining confidence limits. Go deeper only for a material
ambiguity, a safety question, a high-value item, or a conflict that would
change truthful copy or price.
